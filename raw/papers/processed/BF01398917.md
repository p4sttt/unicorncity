Summary. We present the convergence analysis of a new domain decomposi-
tion technique for finite element approximations. This technique was intro-
duced in ,11 and is based on an iterative procedure among subdomains
in which transmission conditions at interfaces are taken into account partly
in one subdomain and partly in its adjacent. No global preconditioner is
needed in the practice, but simply single-domain finite element solvers are
required. An optimal strategy for an automatic selection of a relaxation
parameter to be used at interface subdomains is indicated. Applications are
given to both elliptic equations and incompressible Stokes equations.

1. Introduction
There has been a considerable number of recent developments in domain decom-
position techniques for finite element approximations. We refer, e.g., to the
papers by Glowinski et al. t2, Golub and Meyers 15, Dryja 10, Bramble
et at. -2-4, Chan and Resasco 8, Bj~rstad and Widlund I, Glowinski and
Wheeler 14, and to ,13 and the references therein.
Motivations for increasing interest in domain decomposition approach are
various and have been discussed at some length in the existing literature. Among
others, let us mention the possibility to face complicated geometries in a simple
manner, or to deal with different type of equations in different parts of the
physical domain, and even to take advantage of parallel processors in computa-
tion. Most part of domain decomposition methods currently in use are based
on a partition of the physical domain into adjoining, non intersecting subdo-
mains. Then a block elimination procedure reduces the global finite element
system to a sequence of "independent" systems, one on every subdomain, plus
a global system concerning the remaining unknowns pertaining to the interface

boundaries only. The use of a suitable preconditioner may drastically improve
the efficiency of the iterative method which is used to handle the interactions
between the two sets of unknowns.
Another approach, which avoids the solution of a global system and the
construction of a proper preconditioner, has been recently proposed in 18.
This approach is based on an equivalence principle between single-domain and
multidomain formulation of second order elliptic boundary-value-problems.
This suggests the use of an iterative procedure among subdomains, yielding
a sequence of single-domain boundary-value-problems. One simply iterates be-
tween two adjacent subdomains by imposing in one of them the continuity
of the solution, and in the other one the continuity of the normal derivative
in a weak sense. At the limit of the convergence process, transmission conditions
at interfaces are therefore satisfied.
The approach based on this idea is so general that it naturally applies to
other kinds of differential operators (e.g., Navier-Stokes equations) as well as
to several types of finite dimensional approximations. Indeed, it was formerly
applied in 11 for the approximation of Helmholtz operators by the spectral
Chebyshev collocation method.
As mentioned above, the same approach has been pursued in 18 for con-
fq~ning finite element approximations to elliptic boundary value problems. In
this way, the original finite element solution is obtained as the limit of a sequence
of finite element solutions of mixed boundary value problems on each subdo-
main, which can be computed using standard single-domain finite element
solvers. The iterative method can be dramatically accelerated by a proper relaxa-
tion on the solution at subdomain interfaces. With this aim, an optimal strategy
for the automatic selection of the relaxation parameter is indicated.
In this paper we provide a convergence analysis for the iterative method
here described, for both the differential problem and its finite element approxima-
tion. For regions partitioned into two subdomains, we find the interval in which
the relaxation parameter should be chosen to get convergence. Moreover, we
show that the error reduction factor per iteration is independent of the finite
element mesh size.
The outline of the paper is as follows. In Sects. 2 and 3 we prove convergence
of the iterative scheme for the differential problem, while in Sect. 4 we present
the finite element approximation and prove convergence of the discrete iterative
scheme. In Sect. 5 we describe the algorithm and we give a matrix interpretation
of our scheme. We show that if the continuity of the normal derivative across
interfaces is imposed variationally rather than pointwise, then the present meth-
od results in a strict analogy with the one of Bjorstad and Widlund Eli. Precisely,
both approaches amount to solving iteratively the interface system of the Schur
complement matrix, using the same preconditioning. The difference is on the
iterative method which is conjugate gradient in 1 and Richardson here. This
analogy stops holding if the patching of the normal derivatives is done in a
different way (which is the case, e.g., of approximations based on spectral meth-
ods). We stress that the effectiveness of the present method is not affected if
the differential operator fails to be self-adjoint (i.e., by lack of symmetry of
the Schur complement).
